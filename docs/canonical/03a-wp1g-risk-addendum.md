# Risk Register Addendum — WP1G Governance

## R032 — Governance capture
A small authority set could coordinate to abuse permitted operations or social pressure. Mitigation: enumerated capabilities, separation of domains, timelocks, transparency, no in-place G0 changes. OPEN.

## R033 — Emergency-power abuse
Emergency keys could keep the system impaired or selectively deny service. Mitigation: risk-reducing-only capability, automatic expiry, independent renewal, public incident record, short redemption holds. OPEN.

## R034 — Governance deadlock/key loss
Loss of privileged keys could prevent necessary maintenance or recovery. Mitigation: fail privileged changes closed, separate recovery domains, later Phase 4 key-rotation/recovery design. OPEN.

## R035 — Semantic upgrade bypass
A proxy, adapter, provider or role change could alter economics without formally changing the constitution. Mitigation: economic-effect classification and Phase 4 invariant testing. OPEN.

## R036 — Index-contingency capture
Decision-makers could select a favorable successor under the label of equivalence. Mitigation: objective trigger, public equivalence evidence, long review window, no in-place materially different benchmark. OPEN.

## R037 — Legacy-version fragmentation
Voluntary migration can leave multiple versions with separate reserves, liquidity and servicing costs. Mitigation: no forced migration; Phase 2/3/4 must model and operationalize legacy servicing/wind-down. OPEN.

## R038 — Legal-compulsion conflict
Applicable law may require controls that conflict with a minimized-governance or non-confiscation design. Mitigation: Phase 3 legal architecture; no assumption that constitutional text overrides law. OPEN.
