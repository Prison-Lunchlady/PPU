# Publication-Lag Analysis

Fact: CPI's observation month is not its release date. BLS's current calendar lists January 2026 released February 13, February released March 11, and August released September 11. September 2025 publication was delayed from October 15 to October 24. [Calendar](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/cpi.htm), [shutdown FAQ](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cpi-faq.htm).

Interpretation: the proposal's April dates combine January and February averages. Calling this a three-month *reference convention* must not imply every price is exactly three months old. If monthly averages are represented by their midpoints and calendar months approximated equally, the weighted source midpoint is about 2.5 months behind the valuation day. Individual CPI inputs, sampling and imputations add different ages. No single exact economic lag follows from the index formula.

For constant illustrative annual price growth r, a 2.5-month delay corresponds to current/lagged price ratio (1+r)^(2.5/12). At 3%, 10%, 20% and 100%, this is about 0.618%, 2.005%, 3.871% and 15.535% above the lagged level. These are scenario calculations, not CPI backtests or guaranteed errors. Relative purchasing-power shortfall uses 1−1/ratio instead; do not conflate denominators. A sudden 10% price jump before any reflection leaves a previously matched nominal claim with 9.091% less current purchasing power. Freezing can make delay unbounded.

Daily interpolation does not manufacture daily measurement. Monthly M−2 steps shorten average effective lag but produce larger discrete changes. Release-time steps reduce delay further but become event-timing dependent. No empirical user tolerance or hedge cost study establishes that 2.5 months is acceptable. Recommendation: acceptable only as a disclosed delayed contractual standard, conditional on Gate 1B review, Q021 user-scope work and later matching tests. Do not market real-time protection. Reopen Gate 1A if contemporaneous or uninterrupted coverage is essential.

Normal cutoff slack is often several weeks, not24hours from publication. For the assumed existing rule and September2025 actual delayed release on October24, admission before November's October31 cutoff would still qualify; the delay alone need not freeze a plan. The24hour lead is measured from plan month start. Actual delivery finality remains required; scheduled calendar dates are not receipt proof.
