Primary disposition: REVISION REQUIRED; revised submission awaiting primary review.** This document supersedes the impairment-policy recommendation in 01/07/09/17 and earlier completion reports. Other arithmetic and fixed-vintage conventions remain unchanged. It does not approve WP1C or implement holder entitlements.

## Decision and limits

Recommend endpoint carry and prospective recovery for temporary failures, with an explicit latched `BENCHMARK_IMPAIRED` state for demonstrated structural change or sustained impairment symptoms. For structural impairment, do **not** recommend permanent freezing as a satisfactory purchasing-power standard. Retain the last contractual calculation provisionally to avoid inventing a value, prominently mark loss of benchmark coverage, and seek a separately approved policy/version. This preserves an existing number; it does not preserve purchasing power or solve permanent failure.

Neither a contingent true-up nor a substitute index is recommended for automatic adoption on current evidence. They are live policy alternatives, not failed research programs. An unlimited operational hold is still possible under the recommended control layer; a label and review deadline do not eliminate that economic failure. If primary review requires a guaranteed, pre-agreed economic outcome for permanent loss of CPI-U, **this candidate is not sufficient for activation**. Do not paper over that issue by calling the freeze conservative or by approving only its arithmetic.

The recommendation ranks (i) no fabricated measurement or retroactive rewrite, (ii) common transparent reference terms, (iii) scope fidelity to Gate 1A, above automatic continuity during structural failure. That ranking is a contestable monetary judgment, not a consequence of mathematics. It needs explicit Gate 1B acceptance of its limits; current Gate 1A does not grant that acceptance. A binding maximum impairment duration, exit, settlement or migration remedy cannot be invented here: its claims/reserve implications are dependencies for later authorized packages. Their absence is an activation blocker, not authorization to begin them now.

## Facts versus interpretation

- **Fact:** October 2025 CPI survey collection and the monthly headline release were absent; some nonsurvey series were later available. A missing national headline is not zero inflation. [BLS shutdown FAQ](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cpi-faq.htm).
- **Fact:** Treasury used 325.604 for October 2025 TIPS obligations and would retain it even if BLS subsequently published an actual observation. This contractual substitute is not a measured BLS headline. [Treasury announcement](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0324).
- **Fact:** PCE and CPI differ in formula, scope and weights; PCE uses many CPI components. [BEA comparison](https://www.bea.gov/help/faq/555), [BEA source-data explanation](https://www.bea.gov/help/faq/1486). **Inference:** changing the publisher does not necessarily diversify a BLS input outage.
- **Fact:** the 2025 gap affects final chained-CPI estimates as well as later CPI expenditure weights. [BLS CE/CPI notice](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cpi.htm). **Inference:** C-CPI-U is not an independent backup for this common failure.
- **Uncertainty:** no tested replacement has been shown to preserve the approved concept through permanent CPI-U discontinuation; no demand evidence establishes holder acceptance of any of these policies. Numerical examples below are hypothetical policy calculations, not forecasts or calibrated reserve requirements.

## Three specified alternatives

**A — Freeze, with prospective recovery if the same benchmark returns.** Committed paths finish. Later unsupported endpoints carry their last value indefinitely; authentic expected observations restart the original prospective path. There is no payment for the elapsed impairment. “Permanent freeze” means indefinite if recovery never occurs, not a prohibition on recovery. This is the prior candidate, now evaluated rather than presumed acceptable.

**B — Historical finality plus contingent holding-period true-up.** Keep every published target and completed transaction final. A separately specified, signed adjustment is due only for units continuously held from the first frozen month start `a` through a predeclared settlement endpoint `b` (the first NORMAL month start after same-benchmark recovery). To make a concrete deterministic comparator, define `C = q/H × sum_d (T*_d − T_d)` over UTC dates in `[a,b)`, with `H=365` days fixed before activation. `q` counts eligible continuously retained units, with a precommitted lot-depletion rule; incoming units do not acquire past entitlement, outgoing units lose it. `T*` is the counterfactual ordinary fixed-vintage daily path, constructed only if every required original observation exists and is authenticated. One settlement occurs per episode; overlapping days cannot accrue twice. The common target continues its original prospective path independently.

`C` is a contractually chosen time-weighted adjustment, **not an economically unique measure of lost welfare or lost purchasing power**. H=365 is an explicit comparison assumption, not a recommended parameter. Positive and negative differences are treated symmetrically. CPI is an index of prices, not a measure of how much each holder spent or would have spent. A terminal-level top-up `q×(T*_b−T_b)` may be zero after recovery despite months of undercoverage; paying both a full catch-up top-up and retaining the rising common target can double reward the same endpoint movement. The time-weighted comparator makes a different, additional coupon-like promise and must be funded as such.

If a missing original observation never exists (including the 2025 headline gap), B's adjustment remains unquantifiable. A precommitted bridge between known endpoints could force a number but would be an estimated counterfactual, not recovered CPI; it is a materially different variant. No bridge, deadline-to-zero, unilateral cap or debt forgiveness is adopted. Deflation creates a negative adjustment: enforceable collection would require prior agreement and a secured/collectible obligation. Forfeiting a negative obligation by transferring is a valuable exit option; preventing that forfeiture changes the claims design. The formula is a comparison, not a selected WP1C liability ledger, tax rule or mint/redemption mechanism.

**C — Fully precommitted structural-contingency benchmark.** Before any activation, an immutable version must name exactly one fallback series and publisher, seasonal form, vintage, artifact, cutoff, eligibility and no-data rule, linkage date/normalization, trigger, switch timing and return rule. There is no menu available to administrators during a crisis. For a concrete family of comparator contracts: on a qualifying structural trigger, finish committed plans; set switch month `s` to the first not-yet-committed month with eligible fallback evidence. Keep the last contractual endpoint `F`; fix `K` to the fallback's eligible first-vintage reference at `s`; thereafter `T_C(t)=F×K(t)/K`, using the same exact daily arithmetic and lag. This preserves the level at switch, not the CPI-U price concept. No automatic switch back; a later version requires separate approval. If eligible K does not exist, remain impaired; do not select a different backup on demand.

A version instantiated with first-vintage C-CPI-U can be compared with a separately instantiated PCE version; these are alternatives chosen **before** issuance, not live discretion. Neither is selected here. An actual C contract is incomplete until its exact series/artifact and all above rules are fixed and tested. Naming a generic “credible index,” asking a committee to pick one later, or allowing whatever feed is available is **not fully precommitted**. Same-provider C-CPI-U illustrates correlated failure; PCE illustrates scope and input-dependence risk; private sources add coverage, continuity and auditability issues. An immutable rule can be objectively executable yet economically wrong.

## Comparison across required dimensions

To remove remaining discretion from the comparison, use the following concrete **hypothetical** C-CPI-U contingency contract C1, not a recommended/activated PPU rule. Its sole backup is BLS `SUUR0000SA0`, all-items U.S. City Average chained CPI-U NSA, first official release vintage only. The [BLS series documentation](https://www.bls.gov/help/hlpforma.htm) identifies that series. Required monthly artifact is the initial BLS CPI release's chained-index table, archived before cutoff; absence or conflicting original artifacts blocks admission. Later quarterly/final revisions never overwrite it. C1 inherits the same UTC cutoff, exact interpolation, 18-place rounding, two authenticated delivery paths and contradiction rule as the core research candidate.

C1 activates only following an authenticated BLS notice explicitly discontinuing CUUR0000SA0 without continued publication of the same series; notice receipt must precede the monthly cutoff. Temporary missing releases, price volatility, an operator's declaration or a generic materiality concern cannot activate C1. This deliberately narrow objective publisher-event trigger may **never** fire even during permanent practical failure. Adding a six-month absence trigger would be an alternative precommitted operational definition, not proof of structural failure, and is not part of C1. Both observations for s−3 and s−2 of the fixed backup must be authenticated before the switch cutoff; otherwise wait and remain impaired. Set `K=backup(s−3)` and F to the endpoint of the last committed original plan; normal backup interpolation runs from s−3 to s−2 in month s. Future missing backup endpoints carry, recovery is prospective and signed, and authenticated backup corrections or discontinuation hold new backup plans. No third series and no automatic CPI-U return exist. Thus C1 is completely precommitted in policy terms but still assumes authenticated source facts and a preactivation archive configuration. It does not solve oracle implementation.

For B's comparable ownership rule, use `q=max(0, opening eligible units − cumulative outgoing units)` over `[a,b)`, rounded in unchanged underlying unit granularity; incoming units never replenish eligibility. A transfer, including to another address controlled by the same person, counts as outgoing for this hypothetical rule. It is mechanically testable with complete transaction history but does not prove continuously held beneficial claims. Choosing a custodian/beneficial-owner rather than unit-address interpretation is a substantive different contract, not a hidden implementation detail. These rules make both comparators concrete enough to falsify without selecting either for PPU.

## Adversarial numerical examples

For 100 continuously retained units, 90 daily observations of T=1 and a hypothetical counterfactual T*=1.10, A has no additional payment and each dollar buys `1/1.10` of the reference basket: a 9.0909% shortfall against that counterfactual. B with H=365 gives `100×90×0.10/365 = 2.465753...` dollars, not the 10-dollar terminal difference. H=90 instead gives 10 dollars. Neither H follows from CPI itself. With T*=0.90, the same rule gives −2.465753...; a transfer that extinguishes the debt lets holders evade it. Keeping a debit attached to the unit instead shifts it to buyers and contradicts a strictly continuously-held-only rule unless disclosed and designed in advance.

For a backup rising 2% while CPI-U rises 10%, a level-1 C target becomes 1.02 against a CPI-based 1.10 counterfactual: `(1−1.02/1.10)=7.2727...%` undercoverage. If the backup falls 2% while CPI rises 10%, undercoverage becomes 10.9090...%. Publishing a new number is not proof of a better monetary promise.

Failure tests: A cannot bound inflation loss for any finite fixed timeout without an additional economic remedy. B cannot infer a never-measured month from a later release, and signed symmetry plus frictionless transferable claims cannot be assumed collectible. C cannot be both guaranteed CPI-U-continuous and automatically switch to an economically different benchmark without a qualification. These are reasons to retain review gates, not proofs that A dominates.

## Proposed impairment states

Use `benchmark_status` separately from NORMAL/CATCH_UP/FROZEN arithmetic modes:

| State | Entry / meaning | Exit / numeric effect |
|---|---|---|
| NOT_ACTIVE | Original initialization not satisfied | Existing genesis rules; no active monetary target |
| NORMAL | No active impairment incident and ordinary plan with necessary data health | Existing path remains unchanged |
| TEMPORARY_DATA_FAILURE | Active target with missing/stale/held delivery, non-NORMAL plan, or unresolved adverse evidence, before structural/persistence trigger | May recover under original rules; not an assertion the institution is healthy |
| BENCHMARK_IMPAIRED | Authenticated discontinuation or material concept-change notice, OR existing 3 consecutive frozen / 3 non-NORMAL in trailing 6 plan threshold | Latched. No automatic substitute, true-up, reset, haircut, redemption halt or governance grant. Underlying path still follows existing finality/carry rules. |

Two cause classes must be reported: `STRUCTURAL_CONFIRMED` for an authenticated relevant official notice, and `PERSISTENT_UNRESOLVED` for time/history symptoms. A threshold proves persistent operational impairment, **not permanent statistical failure**. An authentication dispute and a correction hold can trigger the latter even if BLS is functioning normally. Three/six is retained from the prior candidate for a narrow revision, not newly claimed as an optimal boundary; one/two would escalate sooner, six/twelve later, and none bounds loss.

Official notices require validated relevance, a stable incident ID, source evidence hash and finalized UTC receipt time. A notice opens the existing index hold at receipt; committed numeric plans are not cancelled. Routine within-concept methodological maintenance is not a structural notice. `SAME_BENCHMARK_RESTORED` may clear that incident's numeric hold only on authenticated restoration of the same approved benchmark and original terms; it does not itself clear the impairment label. No substitute can satisfy this event. A `CLEAR_IMPAIRMENT` review event must name the exact incident and have validated evidence, same-benchmark confirmation, two started consecutive NORMAL monthly plans committed after that incident, and no remaining active index hold or authenticated conflict at that event time. Fresh monitoring is still separately required for data health. Every incident must be cleared; equal-time openings take precedence. Restore/clear events are logged prospectively; no rewritten history, backdated input or omission of the earlier incident.

`BENCHMARK_IMPAIRED` forces the existing necessary data-eligibility flag false even if the numeric path has recovered. A true flag never authorizes minting; a false flag is not a new redemption or transfer restriction. The two-normal-plan recovery criterion is a proposed anti-flapping control, not a proven economic threshold; it may extend impaired status unnecessarily. General governance, who can authenticate/dispose, and enforceability remain later dependencies.

## Clearance-time evidence and finality refinement

A CLEAR_IMPAIRMENT event archives the clean monitoring attestations and adverse-free fact accepted at its own finalized instant. Replay validates those event-time facts, never a later query's latest monitor timestamps or adverse flag. Later monitoring failure can create current TEMPORARY_DATA_FAILURE without retrospectively undoing a valid clearance. Historical authenticated packet conflicts and holds are still checked at clearance time. Missing, stale or unvalidated clearance evidence fails closed. This distinction fixes a defect found during revision review; four regression checks cover event-time versus query-time evidence.
