# Oracle Timing and Staleness Requirements

This specifies required behavior, not a blockchain, committee, software contract or oracle vendor.

Three clocks must be separate: BLS observation month, official publication instant/edition, finalized receipt in the protocol log. A BLS release does not itself change that day's PPU target. Required observation M−2 affects month M only if qualifying admissions precede C(M); its first numerical increment binds on M's second day at 00:00 UTC, while its monthly plan is committed at C(M). The first day remains S. At equality with cutoff admission is late.

BLS releases commonly say 08:30 Eastern. That is 13:30 UTC in standard time and 12:30 UTC in daylight time; the date-specific timezone conversion must be recorded. [BLS August 2025 release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_09112025.htm), [NIST timezone FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/pml/time-and-frequency-division/local-time-faqs). BLS may change dates; release calendars are monitoring inputs, not substitutes for actual publication evidence or changes to fixed cutoffs.

Require at least two distinct configured delivery paths, original-publication capture, exact fields/base/month match, trustworthy finalized receipt time and immutable historical queries. Mirrored APIs sharing a common backend are not automatically independent. A content hash proves identity of captured bytes, not publisher authenticity. Conflicting authentic first-edition claims block; official correction editions trigger review instead. A single malformed/spoofed abnormal input is quarantined. Two valid agreeing paths can admit despite an unauthenticated bad third path, but two matching compromised paths can defeat this minimum: threshold choice alone is not a security proof.

Monitoring attestations must cover notices, source access and expected release status at least daily. The necessary issuance flag requires two clean attestations younger than 24 hours and no unresolved adverse event. Source absence at an announced release time blocks on recorded detection; an unreported incident is detectable only through monitoring. Stale health cannot increase issuance eligibility. Restoring monitoring cannot alter historical targets or make a FROZEN/CATCH_UP month issuance-eligible. This flag leaves all other mint conditions unspecified.

Chain downtime after plan cutoff: preserve the precommitted mathematical schedule; block executable issuance without current health. Downtime spanning a cutoff: if qualifying evidence was not finalized before cutoff, that month's endpoint freezes. On recovery, no use of a source's earlier publication timestamp as if it were earlier protocol receipt. A later chain must establish finality, timestamp bounds and outage semantics sufficient to enforce this requirement; absent that, the specification is not deployable.

Security limits to retain: common-source compromise, plausible false CPI, censorship near cutoff, denial of service through genuine conflict, unnoticed institutional change, forged monitor attestations and consensus-history failure. A numerical reasonableness filter cannot reliably distinguish hyperinflation from an attack and therefore must not silently cap authenticated CPI. Operational authenticity and anomaly review remain open implementation work.
